 # 1. Use Case Blog

Evaluate this Use Case Blog using only the six parameters below. Each parameter must be evaluated specifically in the context of a real business use case. Do not evaluate it as a generic blog post.

### 1. Authenticity

Check whether the use case feels grounded in a real business situation. Look for a believable company/problem context, realistic users or teams, actual constraints, specific workflows, decisions, challenges, and outcomes. Penalize situations that feel invented, overly perfect, vague, or constructed only to demonstrate a product capability. A high score requires details that make the situation feel genuinely experienced.

### 2. Practitioner Depth

Check whether the article explains how the problem was actually approached and handled. Look for operational details, decisions made, implementation considerations, obstacles, trade-offs, process changes, and what happened in practice. Penalize articles that only describe the problem and solution at a high level without explaining how the solution worked in the real workflow.

### 3. Original Insight

Check whether the use case provides a meaningful lesson that goes beyond the obvious statement that the solution solved a problem. Look for unexpected findings, specific lessons, useful observations, decision-making insights, or conclusions that another practitioner could learn from. Penalize generic best practices that could appear in any use case.

### 4. Writing Voice

Check whether the article sounds like someone who understands and has experienced the business problem firsthand. The voice should feel natural, specific, confident, and practitioner-oriented rather than corporate, promotional, or mechanically generated. Penalize excessive formality, repetitive phrasing, exaggerated claims, and generic AI-style language.

### 5. Learning Evidence

Check whether the article gives the reader enough concrete evidence to learn from the use case. Look for examples, before-and-after situations, measurable outcomes, workflow details, mistakes, lessons, decisions, or specific observations. A high score requires evidence that allows the reader to understand what actually happened and what can be applied elsewhere.

### 6. AI Fluff

Check how much of the article consists of content that sounds useful but does not add meaningful information. Look for generic introductions, unnecessary transitions, buzzwords, repeated explanations, obvious observations, inflated claims, and conclusions that simply restate earlier points. Score this parameter negatively: 1 = very little fluff, 10 = pervasive AI-like filler.


# 2. Platform Hub Content

Evaluate this Platform Hub Content specifically as content intended to explain a platform and how it is used. Do not evaluate it as a generic product article or feature description.

### 1. Authenticity

Check whether the content reflects genuine understanding of how the platform is used in real business environments. Look for realistic workflows, user roles, business situations, platform usage patterns, and practical constraints. Penalize generic software descriptions that could apply to almost any platform.

### 2. Practitioner Depth

Check whether the article explains how different platform capabilities actually support a business workflow. Look for relationships between capabilities, modules, data, users, processes, configuration, implementation, and operational usage. Penalize feature lists that describe what the platform has without explaining how those capabilities work together in practice.

### 3. Original Insight

Check whether the article provides platform-level understanding that is not obvious from a basic feature description. Look for meaningful connections between capabilities, workflows, teams, data, and business outcomes. Penalize content that simply repeats standard product positioning or feature benefits.

### 4. Writing Voice

Check whether the writing sounds like an informed platform expert explaining how the platform is useful. It should be clear and confident without becoming sales-heavy. Penalize excessive promotional language, repeated claims about platform power, generic technology language, and overly polished AI-style phrasing.

### 5. Learning Evidence

Check whether the article gives concrete ways for the reader to understand or apply the platform. Look for workflows, examples, scenarios, capability combinations, practical situations, comparisons, or demonstrated outcomes. A high score requires more than statements that the platform is flexible, powerful, integrated, or easy to use.

### 6. AI Fluff

Check for generic product language that adds little information: broad claims, repetitive benefits, buzzwords, feature introductions with no substance, unnecessary transitions, and repeated summaries. Score negatively: 1 = minimal fluff, 10 = pervasive fluff.

Score each parameter from 1–10 and return the result in Markdown using the same six-row evaluation table, followed by Total Score, Overall Assessment, and Priority Improvements.

# 3. Industry Hub Content

Evaluate this Industry Hub Content specifically for how convincingly and deeply it addresses the stated industry. Do not reward the article simply for repeatedly mentioning the industry name.

### 1. Authenticity

Check whether the article demonstrates genuine familiarity with the industry's environment. Look for realistic terminology, workflows, stakeholders, responsibilities, constraints, problems, regulations or operational realities where relevant. Penalize generic business content where the industry could be replaced with another industry without changing the substance.

### 2. Practitioner Depth

Check whether someone working in the industry would recognize the operational detail as credible. Look for industry-specific processes, decisions, metrics, roles, dependencies, challenges, and practical consequences. Penalize high-level statements that describe industry problems without explaining how those problems actually occur.

### 3. Original Insight

Check whether the article offers a distinctive interpretation of an industry challenge, opportunity, trend, or operational problem. Look for less-obvious observations, useful connections, specific implications, or insights that go beyond commonly repeated industry statements.

### 4. Writing Voice

Check whether the article sounds knowledgeable about the industry rather than like generic corporate content with industry terminology inserted. The voice should be clear, informed, and natural. Penalize excessive jargon, formal filler, repetitive statements, and generic AI-generated phrasing.

### 5. Learning Evidence

Check whether the article supports its industry discussion with concrete examples, scenarios, data, observations, outcomes, metrics, or practical situations. Evidence should help the reader understand how the issue manifests in the industry, not merely assert that the issue exists.

### 6. AI Fluff

Check for generic statements that could apply to almost any industry, broad introductions, buzzwords, repetitive explanations, obvious observations, and empty claims about transformation or innovation. Score negatively: 1 = very little fluff, 10 = pervasive fluff.


# 4. KPI / Metrics Content

Evaluate this content specifically as KPI or metrics-focused content. Judge whether it helps a practitioner understand and use the metric, rather than simply defining it.

### 1. Authenticity

Check whether the KPI is presented in a realistic measurement and decision-making context. Look for situations where the metric would actually be tracked, discussed, compared, or acted upon. Penalize dictionary-style definitions or artificial examples that have no meaningful business context.

### 2. Practitioner Depth

Check whether the article explains the KPI sufficiently for someone to use it correctly. Look for how it is calculated, what data is required, where the data comes from, how frequently it should be measured, what influences the value, how it should be interpreted, its limitations, related metrics, and what actions it can inform. Penalize content that provides only a definition and formula.

### 3. Original Insight

Check whether the article reveals something useful that is not obvious from the KPI's definition. Look for interpretation pitfalls, hidden drivers, misleading readings, relationships with other metrics, trade-offs, segmentation considerations, or situations where the KPI can be misunderstood.

### 4. Writing Voice

Check whether the KPI is explained in the voice of an experienced analyst or practitioner. The explanation should be precise and easy to understand without sounding like a textbook or automatically generated definition. Penalize repetitive explanations, unnecessary terminology, and formula-heavy writing without interpretation.

### 5. Learning Evidence

Check whether the article demonstrates the KPI through numbers or realistic scenarios. Look for worked calculations, examples, sample data, interpretation of results, comparisons, or practical decision scenarios. A high score requires evidence that allows the reader to understand how the metric behaves and how to use it.

### 6. AI Fluff

Check for repeated definitions, generic statements about becoming “data-driven,” filler around formulas, unnecessary explanations of obvious benefits, inflated claims about the importance of the metric, and repeated conclusions. Score negatively: 1 = minimal fluff, 10 = pervasive fluff.

# 6. Guide / Framework

Evaluate this content specifically as a Guide or Framework. The key question is whether the reader receives a usable method rather than a collection of general advice.

### 1. Authenticity

Check whether the proposed guide or framework appears grounded in real practice. Look for realistic constraints, decisions, trade-offs, mistakes, situations, or experiences that explain why the method exists. Penalize frameworks that feel artificially constructed from generic advice.

### 2. Practitioner Depth

Check whether the framework can actually guide someone through the problem. Look for clear stages, actions, decision points, criteria, dependencies, exceptions, inputs, outputs, and explanations of when to use each part. Penalize frameworks that use impressive labels but provide little operational guidance.

### 3. Original Insight

Check whether the framework contains a genuinely useful way of thinking about the problem. Look for distinctive sequencing, categorization, decision logic, models, principles, or connections that provide more value than a standard checklist.

### 4. Writing Voice

Check whether the content sounds like an experienced practitioner teaching someone how to approach the problem. It should be direct, instructional, and practical. Penalize motivational filler, excessive formal language, generic coaching language, and AI-style signposting.

### 5. Learning Evidence

Check whether the framework is demonstrated through examples, scenarios, decisions, checklists, criteria, applications, or lessons. A high score requires enough evidence for the reader to understand how the framework behaves when applied to a real situation.

### 6. AI Fluff

Check for generic advice, motivational statements, obvious recommendations, excessive section introductions, repetitive explanations, unnecessary summaries, and buzzwords that do not improve the framework. Score negatively: 1 = minimal fluff, 10 = pervasive fluff.


# 7. Success Story

Evaluate this content specifically as a Success Story. Judge whether the story feels like a credible account of a real customer/business outcome rather than promotional copy presented as a story.

### 1. Authenticity

Check whether the customer context, initial situation, problem, implementation, and outcome feel believable and specific. Look for concrete business circumstances, real constraints, identifiable changes in the way work was performed, and realistic outcomes. Penalize stories that feel manufactured, overly perfect, vague, or designed only to praise the solution.

### 2. Practitioner Depth

Check whether the story explains what actually changed operationally. Look for implementation decisions, affected teams, workflow changes, challenges, adoption issues, process changes, and how the solution was incorporated into existing work. Penalize stories that jump directly from “problem” to “successful result” without explaining what happened in between.

### 3. Original Insight

Check whether the story provides a specific lesson or business insight beyond “the customer used the solution and achieved better results.” Look for unexpected findings, lessons from implementation, changes in decision-making, process insights, or observations that other organizations could learn from.

### 4. Writing Voice

Check whether the story feels credible, human, and evidence-led. The tone should communicate the customer's experience without sounding like exaggerated advertising. Penalize excessive praise, superlatives, polished promotional language, and repetitive statements about success.

### 5. Learning Evidence

Check whether the success claim is supported by concrete evidence. Look for before-and-after metrics, measurable improvements, timelines, process changes, specific outcomes, customer observations, or other verifiable details. Do not reward invented precision or assume evidence that the article does not provide.

### 6. AI Fluff

Check for generic success language such as exaggerated transformation claims, repetitive praise, phrases describing the solution as revolutionary or game-changing without evidence, empty statements about impact, and overly polished case-study language. Score negatively: 1 = minimal fluff, 10 = pervasive fluff.


# 8. Offline Marketing Content

Evaluate this content specifically as Offline Marketing Content such as a brochure, one-pager, event handout, print advertisement, flyer, or other physical marketing asset. Do not judge it using the standards of a long-form article.

### 1. Authenticity

Check whether the marketing message is grounded in a real audience problem and a credible value proposition. Look for specific business situations, genuine customer needs, realistic claims, and concrete value. Penalize generic advertising language that could describe almost any product or company.

### 2. Practitioner Depth

Check whether the content demonstrates enough understanding of the buyer's actual situation to make the message credible. Look for practical use cases, workflow relevance, common challenges, objections, decision factors, and how the solution fits into the user's environment. Penalize content that consists only of slogans and broad benefits.

### 3. Original Insight

Check whether the central marketing idea or message has a distinctive angle. Look for a memorable observation, specific problem framing, useful point of view, or differentiated message that separates the content from generic B2B marketing.

### 4. Writing Voice

Check whether the language is concise, confident, persuasive, and appropriate for an offline marketing format. It should communicate value quickly without sounding exaggerated or machine-generated. Penalize excessive adjectives, corporate jargon, long explanations, generic slogans, and unnatural promotional phrasing.

### 5. Learning Evidence

Check whether important claims are supported by concrete proof. Look for customer outcomes, numbers, facts, specific use cases, examples, proof points, or other evidence that gives credibility to the marketing message. Penalize unsupported claims that rely entirely on persuasive wording.

### 6. AI Fluff

Check for empty slogans, buzzwords, generic benefit lists, inflated adjectives, repetitive calls to action, vague transformation claims, and filler that occupies space without communicating meaningful value. Score negatively: 1 = minimal fluff, 10 = pervasive fluff.
